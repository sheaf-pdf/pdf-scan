## Features closed by S0 slice 9

- **FEAT-14** · scan-worker · S0 · done — **Windows workers in a Job object that ends them with the app, and a tree kill.** NAPS2's practice (study only) and `crates/scan-worker.md`'s hygiene: each worker assigned to a Job object made with `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE`, so the app's end, a crash among them, ends the worker and every process a driver started in it. S0 slice 4 left it out (its decision 22): it takes calls `std` doesn't make, so `unsafe` or a crate, and the crates vetted fall short today: `process-wrap` 10.0.1 (MIT OR Apache-2.0, `rust-analyzer`'s and `watchexec`'s) makes its `std` job without kill-on-close (`make_job_object(handle, false)` in its `src/std/job_object.rs`; only its tokio side's `KillOnDrop` sets it), and `win32job` 2.0.3 (kill-on-close) is on `windows` 0.61, beside the 0.62 the `wia` backend will take. Meanwhile a worker exits at the end of its input and aborts 10 s later should a driver's call hang (an abort Windows Error Reporting may report), and a process a driver started outlives both. On Unix each worker leads a process group of its own already, so a group kill there is this item's other half, should a driver be seen to start processes. Built in slice 9, where Windows drivers are tried, with the 32-bit worker. **Done:** S0 slice 9 (`slices/s0-9-twain-backend.md`, step 4), in `/crates/scan-worker/src/sys.rs` on `windows-sys` 0.61: each Windows worker assigned at spawn to an unnamed Job Object configured with `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` (tolerating fast-exit `ERROR_ACCESS_DENIED` races when `child.try_wait()` shows the child already exited), and terminated via `TerminateJobObject` before reaping on kill, watchdog timeout, or `StopHandle::stop`, tested in `tests/stop_handle.rs` against a worker whose driver spawns a grandchild holding its pipes. *(S0 slice 4, decisions 12 and 22, and its dependencies weighed)*
